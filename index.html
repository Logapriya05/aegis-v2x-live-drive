How much the vehicle can trust its situation right now. Six hazards are each scored from 0 (none) to 1 (extreme) and combined: collision risk from time-to-collision, a pedestrian ahead within stopping distance, a red light that needs hard braking, close following (gap under 1.5 s), lane status (YELLOW / RED) and low twin trust. The last one comes from the calibrated Digital Twin trust T of TwinTrust-AP: none while T ≥ 0.6, rising to 0.6 as T falls to 0, so a doubtful twin alone gives at most Danger, never Critical. Trust Score = 5 + 95 × (1 − r₁)(1 − r₂)…(1 − r₆), so several moderate hazards lower it more than one alone. Only a confirmed collision gives 0; it then recovers over about 10 s. Driving safety is the same score without the twin term. Labels: Safe ≥ 75 · Caution 50–75 · Danger 25–50 · Critical below 25. Selected vehicle uses collision risk with the ego, its lane status and the trust of its own twin entry.

    Lane uses the road geometry (14 m roads, two 3.5 m lanes each way, double centre line, right-hand traffic) and the vehicle's true position, heading, width and lane-change state. GREEN: in a permitted lane, correct direction (lane changes over the dashed divider and turns inside junctions are permitted). YELLOW: within 0.3 m of the centre line or road edge, straddling lanes for more than 4 s, or heading across the road. RED: across the centre line, on the wrong side, past the road edge or off the road. Trust: YELLOW below 50%, RED when the source is isolated (below 35%). Collision: YELLOW when contact with the ego is predicted within 3 s, RED within 1.5 s or after contact. Overall is the worst of the three, but each stays visible on its own.

    Per-source trust

    V2X message reliability, checked against ego LiDAR—
    SourceLinkDistLatencyEvidenceTrustTrust ScoreStatus

    α ← λʷα + w·c, β ← λʷβ + w(1−c), λ = 0.97. c is the agreement between the claimed position and the ego's LiDAR (w = 1), or with the source's own previous message when the source is out of LiDAR range (w = 0.25). Below 0.35 a source is isolated and the twin places it from LiDAR. Isolation uses data trust only, because an honest but erratic driver still reports accurate positions. Behaviour trust uses the same update with its own α, β (prior 0.95), fed by the lane status of the LiDAR-observed position: GREEN c = 1, w = 0.1; YELLOW c = 0.4, w = 0.2; RED c = 0, w = 0.6, counted after 3 consecutive identical checks. Lane changes and turns announced by the vehicle's indicator (CAM exterior-light field) are permitted. No behaviour check happens beyond LiDAR range or when LiDAR is degraded. Shown trust = data × behaviour. This is the demo's trust heuristic, not the calibrated TwinTrust estimator.

      Twin coordinate frame

      The twin uses a right-handed ground frame. X points east and Y points north (up on the mini-map). The rulers along the road you are on show the coordinate along that road in metres, with ticks every 5 m and labels every 20 m. Lane markings use the same 3.5 m lane width as the road view.

      Every object in the twin is placed from a received V2X message, or from the ego's LiDAR when the source has no radio or has been isolated. Buildings, roads and signals are the static map, generated from the same layout as the road view.

      Twin trust evidence

      Criticality features

      What happens every cycle. The ego compares each digital-twin object with where its own LiDAR sees it. That gives the evidence for the Calibrated Twin Trust Estimator: prediction error e, error spread u, information age a and link quality q. T = σ(a·raw + b), with raw = 0.20q − 0.35e − 0.25u − 0.20a. Criticality C is the weighted sum of the five features on the right, and GDCA authority A = exp(−½z²) from each object's residuals against their robust spread. TAHS turns T, C and A into a prediction horizon H, the FSDP table picks one action for the current (trust, criticality) state, and the beam is switched in the same cycle when buildings block V2V links, so prediction, synchronisation and beam are decided jointly. Formulas are Vaishnavi's Python code, ported exactly (12,000 test cases match). The twin error uses the simulator, which a real vehicle would only see through its own sensors.

      since the last mode switch

      J = 0.25·info age + 0.20·energy + 0.20·sync overhead + 0.20·beam failures − 0.15·twin correctness (configs/model.yaml). Lower is better. Switch between the two policies under the same faults to compare them. In recorded test runs (8 conditions × 150 s, mmWave beam model) this table cut J by 30% against the fixed policy (0.090 vs 0.128) and kept information fresher, but its twin was correct 3.5 points less often (51.2% vs 54.7%). Details in research/README.md.

      Trust calibration

      Platt scaling, held-out cycles

      Calibrated means T matches how often the twin is really right. Target: a twin object is within 1 m of where LiDAR sees it on the next cycle. Fitted on recorded cycles from this simulated city, not from CARLA.
